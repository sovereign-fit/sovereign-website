Frequently Asked Questions

How does sovereign AI ensure data residency compliance? +

Because inference and fine-tuning execute on hardware inside your declared jurisdiction, residency is enforced at the physical and network layers rather than by contractual promise. Auditable network egress policies and region-pinned storage complete the control chain for GDPR, Schrems II, and national sovereignty mandates.

What hardware is required to run private LLMs on-premises? +

Production 70B-class deployments typically begin with 2–4 NVIDIA H100 or A100 nodes with NVLink, while quantized 7B–13B models serve well on a single GPU workstation. We size clusters against your concurrency, latency, and context-window requirements during the architecture briefing.

Does air-gapped deployment introduce unacceptable model latency? +

Modern serving stacks (vLLM with continuous batching and speculative decoding) deliver first-token latencies comparable to hosted APIs for enterprise workloads. Because inference runs on dedicated — not oversubscribed — hardware, p99 latency is frequently more predictable than public endpoints.

Is fine-tuning on proprietary data private? +

Yes. Fine-tuning runs entirely within your perimeter on open-weight checkpoints. No dataset, gradient artifact, or derived weight ever transits a third-party system, and the resulting adapter or merged model is an asset your organization controls outright.
